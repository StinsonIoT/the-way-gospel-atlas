Accuracy. Dots mark named towns at approximate locations. Dashed circles mark regions where the exact site is unknown. Dashed lines are schematic and never follow modern roads; grey dots mean the text moves on without describing the travel. Hollow diamonds are traditional or later-proposed sites. The Jerusalem plan is a simplified reconstruction over real terrain. It shows only the first wall: the second wall’s course is debated, and the third wall was begun by Agrippa I (41–44 CE), after the Gospel events. Pilate’s praetorium is marked at both proposed sites, Herod’s palace and the Antonia.

Scripture quotations: Berean Standard Bible (public domain). Map data: Natural Earth (public domain), modern coastlines. Terrain: Terrain Tiles on AWS (Mapzen; SRTM and other public sources). Built with MapLibre GL JS and maplibre-contour.

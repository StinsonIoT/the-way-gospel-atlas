# Historical and Biblical Accuracy Standard

## Primary narrative sources
Matthew, Mark, Luke, John; Acts 1 for ascension. Use BSB for quotations after verifying wording and permissions. Passage references must be supplied per event.

## Evidence fields
Each event/place must identify:
- What the passage explicitly states.
- Geographic/archaeological support and source.
- Traditional identification.
- Scholarly proposal or reconstruction.
- Remaining uncertainty.

Suggested confidence terms: identified, approximate, region-only, traditional, disputed, unknown. Confidence must not be implied by a point's apparent precision.

## Mapping rules
- No invented roads, intermediate stops, dates, building footprints, exact event coordinates, or harmonized Gospel chronology.
- A recorded town-to-town movement does **not** prove the specific path. Distinguish recorded movement from proposed route geometry.
- Flight to Egypt: regional destination, not invented city or itinerary.
- Distinguish Bethany near Jerusalem from Bethany across the Jordan; do not conflate similar names.
- Unnamed mountains (sermon/transfiguration/post-resurrection meeting) remain unnamed; traditional associations are labeled as such.
- Bethsaida, feeding sites, eastern shore Gerasene/Gadarene sites, Sychar/Jacob's well and Emmaus require explicit qualification.
- Holy Week day assignments can be conventional or debated; do not flatten differences among Gospel accounts.
- Resurrection appearances are not evidence of ordinary mapped travel by Jesus between them.
- Ascension: preserve Luke 24 and Acts 1 geographic wording rather than imposing one exact point.

## Jerusalem
Represent early first-century Jerusalem, not a modern city plan. Distinguish Temple precinct, Kidron Valley, Mount of Olives, period-appropriate walls/gates, pools, southern approaches, possible high-priestly areas, Pilate trial locations, Gethsemane, Golgotha, tomb and Last Supper traditions. Proposed wall lines and architectural reconstructions need provenance and explicit visual uncertainty. Do not present modern Via Dolorosa as the established path.

## Data/source review
For each mapped geometry record stable ID, feature type, passage references, identification confidence, source URLs/publications, date of review, geometry method, and notes. Record licenses/attributions. For disputed places prefer candidate sites or uncertainty areas over falsely precise single markers.

## Unresolved research examples
Bethsaida identification; Emmaus candidates; Jerusalem wall phases and Pilate trial location; Golgotha/tomb identifications; sites of feeding miracles and eastern-shore encounters; chronology of Holy Week. Record consequences for rendering, labels and routes before asset approval.

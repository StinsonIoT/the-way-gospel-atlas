Sources

Where the evidence comes from.

Every claim in the atlas rests on Scripture, an ancient writer, an excavation or a published map source. This page gathers them in one place.

Scripture

  • The Berean Standard Bible (BSB)All quotations. Public domain. Passages open in the atlas through the Free Use Bible API (bible.helloao.org) and on BibleHub.
  • The four Gospels and Acts 1Matthew, Mark, Luke and John supply every scene; Acts 1 supplies the ascension and the closing words.
  • Other books cited in the notesLeviticus 12–13 (offerings and skin disease), Psalm 118 (the festival psalm), Micah 5:2 (Bethlehem), and 1 Maccabees 4 (the rededication of the temple).

Ancient writers

  • Josephus, Jewish Antiquities (about AD 93)John the Baptist at Machaerus (18.116–119); women’s testimony (4.219); the death of James, brother of Jesus (20.200); Herod’s last years and the execution of his sons; Pilate’s residence at Caesarea.
  • Josephus, The Jewish War (about AD 75)Descriptions of the temple, Herod’s palace and the walls of Jerusalem used for the 3D model (book 5).
  • Tacitus, Annals 15.44 (about AD 116)Christ executed under Pontius Pilate in the reign of Tiberius.
  • The Mishnah (compiled about AD 200)The water-drawing and lamps at the Feast of Tabernacles (Sukkah 4–5); mourners and flute players at funerals (Ketubot 4:4); temple courts and dimensions (Middot); betrothal customs.
  • Justin Martyr, Dialogue with Trypho 78 (mid-second century)The early tradition of a cave at Bethlehem.

Excavations and inscriptions

  • The Pilate Stone, Caesarea (found 1961)Inscription naming Pontius Pilate, prefect of Judea.
  • The Caiaphas ossuary, Jerusalem (found 1990)Burial box inscribed “Joseph son of Caiaphas”.
  • The temple warning inscriptions (first found 1871)Greek notices forbidding foreigners to pass into the inner courts.
  • The heel bone of a crucified man, Jerusalem (found 1968)Iron nail still in the bone; buried in a family tomb.
  • The Galilee boat (found 1986)A wooden fishing boat of about the first century, recovered near Magdala.
  • The Magdala synagogue (found 2009)A first-century synagogue with a carved stone showing the temple menorah.
  • The Pool of Siloam (found 2004)A Second Temple–period pool at the south end of the ancient city.
  • The Pool of BethesdaTwo large pools north of the temple, divided by a central wall.
  • CapernaumFranciscan excavations of the village, the later limestone synagogue over an earlier basalt floor, and the house venerated as Peter’s, with pilgrims’ graffiti.
  • Nazareth (2009)Remains of a house from Jesus’ time near the Church of the Annunciation.
  • Stone-vessel workshops in GalileeIncluding a quarry workshop at Reina, beside Kafr Kanna.
  • Herod’s winter palaces at JerichoPalaces, pools and gardens at New Testament Jericho.
  • Banias (Caesarea Philippi)The cave and rock-cut niches of the shrine of Pan.
  • The Western Wall excavationsStones thrown down from the temple mount in AD 70.
  • Khirbet QanaA cave complex venerated by Byzantine and Crusader pilgrims as Cana.
  • The Garden TombDated by most archaeologists to centuries before Jesus.
  • The Church of the Holy SepulchreAn old quarry with first-century tombs, honored as the site by the fourth century.
